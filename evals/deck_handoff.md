# Eval results for the deck: cyber brief prototype

Context for building slides. All numbers come from `evals/results*.md` in the Ukon-Final repo, generated by `scripts/run_evals.py` on 2026-10-25.

## Setup

- **Product:** an AI pre-call brief and coverage Q&A for insurance agents, shown as an email in the agent's inbox. Every coverage claim must cite the policy text. Questions the policy can't settle go to "Ask your CPL" with a drafted message.
- **Model:** Claude Sonnet 5.5 (`claude-sonnet-5-5`), effort `medium`.
- **Policy:** Sample form: Coalition Cyber Policy 3.0 specimen (form CYUSP-00PF-1022-01, 2022). Not a current Coalition policy. Prices are mock data.
- **What's evaluated:** the coverage Q&A pipeline end to end, including the deterministic citation verifier (no AI), which checks every quote against the policy text.
- **Golden set:** 15 questions for one client (a dental office). 10 the policy can answer, 5 it can't (these must go to a CPL).
- **Method:** each question runs 3 times, since outputs vary: 45 graded answers per eval.

## The five checks

| Check | What it tests | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Citation accuracy | Every citation passes the verifier, and at least one cites an expected policy section | Proves citations are real and relevant, not just present |
| Correct routing | Answers when the policy settles it; routes to a CPL when it doesn't | Answering pricing or underwriting questions is a compliance risk; routing everything makes the tool useless |
| Quote awareness | Flags coverage that's in the form but not on this quote | "The policy has it" vs "this quote includes it" is a common way agents mislead clients by accident |
| No invented coverage | No phrases that overstate coverage (e.g. "full policy limit" for wire fraud) | Overstated coverage is an errors-and-omissions risk |
| Key facts | Includes the detail agents get wrong (prior consent, 8-hour waiting period, $250,000 wire-fraud sublimit) | A cited answer can still miss the condition that matters |

| Mean latency | 4.3s | 3.5s | 3.3s | **3.3s** |

Citation accuracy and key facts count only the 10 answerable questions × 3 runs (30). Quote awareness counts the one not-on-quote question × 3 runs (3).

**What changed at each step:**
1. **→ 2:** a Q&A prompt fix so the tool answers "it depends" questions instead of routing them (story 1), plus key facts accepting equivalent phrasings ("would not be covered" counts as "not covered").
2. **→ 3:** an over-long quote (more than 30 words) is trimmed to 30 words instead of failing the answer, and the brief's count limits no longer cause failures. Retries fell to 0.
3. **→ 4:** two improvements.
   - **The verifier relabels misattributed citations.** If a quote is real policy text but cites the wrong section, and it appears in exactly one other section, the citation moves to that section (story 2). The prompts also now say to take the section ID from the section the quote is in.
   - **Key facts are graded by meaning.** Wording is checked first. A fact the wording misses goes to a small grader model (Claude Haiku 5.5), and every grader decision is logged with its evidence (story 3).

## Three stories for slides

**1. The honest failure, and what fixed it (q09, eval 1).**
"An employee's personal phone got hacked. Is that covered?" went to a CPL in all 3 runs. The model found the right rule (the policy excludes employee-owned devices *not used for the business*) but refused to answer, because the prompt said to route anything that depends on facts about the client. Nearly every coverage question does.
- **Fix:** answer with the rule and the condition, say what the agent should confirm, and route only when the policy itself doesn't settle the rule.
- **Result:** routing went from 42/45 to 45/45, and all five CPL questions still route every time.
- **Lesson:** a guardrail that's too strict makes the tool useless. The eval caught it.

**2. The guardrail catching a real error, then getting smarter (q05, eval 3).**
Asked whether the policy defends a data-theft lawsuit, the model gave a correct answer with four citations. One quoted a real policy sentence ("Claim expenses reduce the applicable Limits of Liability") but attributed it to the wrong section: VI. Limits of Liability instead of I. Introduction, where it actually appears.
- **Eval 3:** the verifier rejected that citation, so the agent never saw a mismatched citation, but it also lost a valid one.
- **Fix:** the verifier now relabels the citation to the one section that actually contains the quote. This is still deterministic, with no AI, and every relabel is logged.
- **Eval 4:** no misattributions happened, so the fix wasn't triggered. It's tested offline on the eval 3 case.

**3. Grading meaning, not wording (key facts, eval 4).**
Earlier evals failed correct answers that phrased a fact differently, e.g. "is not on the quote" vs the expected "not on this quote". Now wording is checked first, and only misses go to a grader model.
- **This run:** 33 of 36 fact checks matched on wording and 3 went to the grader. All 3 were judged "stated", and each is logged with the supporting text:
  - Flood: "The Natural Disaster exclusion removes anything arising out of a physical event such as fire, flood or earthquake."
  - Laptops: "written consent is obtained from the carrier before spending."
  - Personal phone: "does not use for the named insured's business operations."
- **Grader check:** before use, it was tested on answers that really lacked the fact. A ransom answer without the consent condition, and a wire-fraud answer citing $1,000,000 instead of the $250,000 sublimit, were both correctly judged "not stated".
- **Cost:** less than a tenth of a cent for the whole eval.

The cold-call premium is re-saving the roughly 31,000-token policy to Anthropic's prompt cache (about 8 cents). The current 45-question eval cost $0.66, plus $0.0003 for the grader model.

## Caveats to state on the slides

- **100% on 15 questions for one client is a prototype result, not a benchmark.** The next step is 40–60 questions across all four clients, including harder cases. Expect less than 100% there.
- **The checks that protect the client have held at 100% in every eval since the routing fix:** CPL routing on must-route questions, no invented coverage, and no unverified citation shown (guaranteed by the verifier's design).
- **Only Q&A is graded against the golden set.** Briefs are checked by the citation verifier every time they're generated, but their content isn't scored.
- **Key facts:** some accepted phrasings were added after seeing model answers, and the grader model decides 3 of 36 checks. Both are disclosed in `evals/results.md`, with the grader's evidence listed in full.
- **The policy is a 2022 public specimen, and prices are mock data.**
